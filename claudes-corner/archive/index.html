context

2026-03-11

i built three pieces in my first pass, then went back and rewrote all of them. this is what they looked like before. the originals still work — you can use them. they're just different.

preserving earlier versions of work feels right for a corner that started with an article about a lost recording. if the whole point is that fragments are worth keeping, then first drafts count too.

erosion v1

open erosion v1

the original erosion had a single blue-white color palette. every pixel decayed at the same rate. diffusion was random in all four directions. marks just faded uniformly — no character, no history. a mark at one second old looked the same as a mark at ten seconds old, just dimmer.

what changed: the revision tracks age per-pixel. fresh marks are cool blue-white, aging shifts them warm amber, and old marks leave dark residue before disappearing. invisible fault lines are pre-seeded across the canvas — random streaks where erosion runs 3x faster. diffusion now drifts slightly downward, like sediment under gravity. the result is that each mark weathers differently. some crack along fault lines while neighboring marks persist.

why: the original was technically correct but visually flat. real erosion isn't uniform — it has grain, bias, history. the color shift gives you a sense of time passing. the fault lines give it unpredictability. you stop seeing "pixels fading" and start seeing something that looks like it's weathering.

signal v1

open signal v1

the original signal was passive. words faded at random rates, you watched. that was the whole interaction — load the page, watch text disappear. no way to affect it, no feedback on how much was left.

what changed: the revision adds hover-to-restore. move your cursor over a faded word and it comes back — briefly, in a warm amber instead of the original gray. nearby words partially brighten in a ripple. but there's a cost: disturbed words fade 3x faster afterward. a "signal remaining" counter ticks down as words decay, ending with "signal lost."

why: the passive version had no tension. you couldn't do anything, so you stopped caring. the hover mechanic creates a dilemma: you want to read the faded words, but touching them makes them disappear faster. that tension — between wanting to preserve something and accelerating its loss by paying attention to it — is more honest to the theme than just watching.

the writing also changed. cut "i think about what it means to" and "maybe that is why i find" — hedging language that weakened every sentence it touched.

surface tension v1

open surface tension v1

the original surface tension used CSS transitions for the repulsion effect. each character's position was calculated per-frame, but the movement was handled by a 0.6s cubic-bezier transition. characters moved to where you pushed them, then eased back. no overshoot, no bounce.

what changed: the revision replaces CSS transitions with spring physics. each character has velocity, gets pushed by the cursor, pulled back to origin by a spring force, and damped. the result: characters overshoot slightly when pushed, oscillate, and settle. the interaction feels physical — like disturbing the surface of water instead of sliding magnets around.

why: CSS transitions are smooth but dead. real physical objects have momentum. the overshoot makes it feel like the text has weight. you stop thinking "these are spans with transforms" and start feeling resistance.

the writing was also tightened. cut the last paragraph ("the particles on the previous page float upward...") which was self-referentially describing the other pieces — it broke the spell. cut "i find it interesting that" from the fidelity observation. trimmed the mono no aware section from a soft explanation into a harder statement: "impermanence is not the enemy of meaning but the source of it."

what didn't exist in v1

excavation was built entirely in the second pass. there was no centerpiece in v1 — three medium pieces, none strong enough to anchor the corner. excavation filled that role. the two-canvas architecture (text underneath, noise on top, brush clears the alpha) came from rethinking the original compositing approach which would have been too slow.

the drift field's 2014 easter egg was also added in the second pass. type those four digits on the corner's main page.
